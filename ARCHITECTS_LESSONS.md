# Lessons from architects' house plans

Research note, 2026-10-09. The aim is to read well-documented house plans, mostly by well-known
architects, against our house method (`DESIGN_PRINCIPLES.md`, cited as §2 to §8), and to pick
out rules we could add as automated checks on AutoCAD Architecture plans (spaces, walls, doors,
windows, furniture blocks).

**How to read this note**

- Every claim about a specific building has a source URL. Most sources are secondary (museum
  sites, National Register forms, architecture magazines, encyclopedias). Where sources disagree,
  or where I could not confirm a detail, it is marked **(uncertain)**.
- Before relying on a dimension or a room position, check the original drawings or a monograph.
  Web summaries often contradict each other (examples: the Robie House entrance position, the
  Farnsworth kitchen, Fisher House dates).
- Part A covers the houses. Part B is the synthesis: what confirms our method, what adds to it
  or challenges it, and which candidate rules could become checks.

---

## Part A. Houses and authors

### A1. Frank Lloyd Wright: Robie House (Chicago, 1910)

- **Entrance:** hidden. It is reached along a walk and sits under the overhanging upper storey,
  away from the street front (sources disagree on which side: north side, or short side;
  **uncertain**). A low, flat ceiling compresses whoever approaches the door.
- **Sequence:** a compressed entry and stair hall open up into the bright, open main floor
  ("compression and release").
- **Zoning and circulation:** living and dining form one long space, divided only by the massive
  fireplace and the central stair. The chimney is pierced above the hearth so the room reads as
  one continuous space.
- **Lesson:** a transition can be a sequence of heights and light, not only a hall room. The
  living-dining division is a "heavy mass", not a wall with a door: one space lightly articulated
  (our §3).

Sources: https://www.architecture.org/online-resources/buildings-of-chicago/robie-house ,
https://sah-archipedia.org/node/12854 , https://kids.britannica.com/students/article/Robie-House/544309 ,
https://www.wosu.org/classical-101/2014-08-19/decompressed-music-and-the-compression-entryway

### A2. Frank Lloyd Wright: Fallingwater (Mill Run, 1935-37)

- **Entrance:** a compressed entrance opens into a low, broad living room with windows on
  several sides.
- **Circulation:** passages are deliberately dark and narrow (compression) and contrast with the
  larger rooms and terraces (expansion). Some ceilings are about 6 ft 4 in (1.93 m) to push the
  eye outward.
- **Zones by floor:** living room and compact kitchen on the main floor; three small bedrooms on
  the next; study and bedroom above. Terraces cantilever from the living level and from both
  bedroom levels, and take about half of the built area.
- **Lesson:** small bedrooms and generous shared or outdoor space are a deliberate trade. Each
  bedroom level has its own terrace: the private zone has private outdoor space.

Sources: https://www.archdaily.com/60022/ad-classics-fallingwater-frank-lloyd-wright ,
https://www.britannica.com/place/Fallingwater , https://www.designingbuildings.co.uk/wiki/Fallingwater

### A3. Frank Lloyd Wright: Jacobs House I, first Usonian house (Madison, 1936-37)

- **Plan:** L-shaped. One wing holds the large living room, the other the bedrooms and study. The
  kitchen ("workspace") and bathroom sit at the joint of the L, with the dining nook in front.
- **Module:** a 2 x 4 ft grid (610 x 1220 mm) scored in the floor slab; about 1,550 sq ft
  (144 m²). No parlour or reception room: all shared life in one living room.
- **Kitchen:** open to the living space, at the hinge where it can watch both wings.
- **Heights as zoning:** ceilings 7 ft 3 in (2.21 m) over bedrooms and halls, 9 ft 4 in over the
  living room, 11 ft 7 in over the kitchen and bath (the raised kitchen roof gives clerestory
  light and ventilation).
- **Heating:** radiant hot-water pipes in the floor slab (sources differ on the cellar and boiler
  details; **uncertain**).
- **Lessons:** the L puts public and private in separate wings joined at one hinge, with the
  service core (kitchen, bath, plumbing) at that hinge. The bedroom wing is reached by a gallery
  that is also storage: circulation doing a second job.

Sources: https://sah-archipedia.org/node/21266 , https://usonia1.com/description/ ,
https://npgallery.nps.gov/NRHP/GetAsset/NRHP/64500164_text , https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/casa-jacobs-madison

### A4. Le Corbusier: Villa Savoye (Poissy, 1928-31) and the Modulor

- **Five points** (formulated 1926 or 1927, sources differ): pilotis, free plan, free facade,
  ribbon windows, roof garden. The free plan works because columns, not walls, carry the floors.
- **Sequence:** an "architectural promenade" built around a central ramp linking entrance hall,
  salon, terrace and solarium. Ground floor: entrance hall, ramp and stair, garage, staff rooms.
  First floor: master bedroom, two more bedrooms, kitchen, lounge, terraces.
- **Grid:** initially 5 m, reduced to 4.75 m for cost.
- **Service on its own level:** the car arrives under the house; garage and staff rooms are on the
  ground floor, separate from the family floor (our §2 service zone, solved in section rather
  than in plan).
- **Modulor:** a scale of proportions from a 1.83 m man (raised arm 2.26 m, navel 1.13 m), in a
  red series (from 1.13 m) and a blue series (from 2.26 m) related by the golden ratio. Basic
  plot 113, 70, 43 cm (43 + 70 = 113; 113 + 70 = 183).
- **Lesson:** a module can be a set of preferred sizes (a series), not one fixed step.

Sources: https://www.architecturelab.net/villa-savoye-le-corbusier/ ,
https://vielfaltdermoderne.de/en/villa-savoye-2/ , https://archeyes.com/the-villa-savoye-le-corbusier/ ,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modulor

### A5. Mies van der Rohe: Farnsworth House (Plano, 1945-51) and Villa Tugendhat (Brno, 1928-30)

**Farnsworth:**
- One glass room on a raised platform. A freestanding wood-veneered core holds the bathrooms,
  mechanical services and fireplace (whether the kitchen is in the core or a separate element
  along it is **uncertain**: sources differ).
- The core stops short of the ceiling and the facades, so it reads as furniture, and the space
  around it reads as a continuous loop. Zones for sleeping, cooking, dressing, eating and sitting
  are defined by freestanding elements, not rooms.
- **Lesson:** a single-person house can be zoned by objects alone. It is the extreme case of our
  §3 ("a space does not need four walls"), and it shows the limit: there is no acoustic or
  visual privacy at all.

**Tugendhat:**
- Built into a slope on three levels. The **entrance floor at street level** holds a small
  entrance hall, the family bedrooms and services; the master bedroom faces the garden. The
  living floor below is one large hall zoned by a freestanding onyx wall and a curved Macassar
  ebony wall, with glass panes that lower into the floor. Utility and technical rooms on the
  lowest level.
- **Lesson:** the private zone is on the road side here, but **one floor above** the living
  level: separation in section rather than distance from the road. Our §2 siting test measures
  only plan distance and would wrongly flag it.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsworth_House , https://www.architakes.com/the-farnsworth-house-part-2-from-the-hearth-to-the-field/ ,
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Tugendhat , https://www.architecturelab.net/villa-tugendhat-mies-van-der-rohe/

### A6. Adolf Loos: Villa Müller (Prague, 1928-30), the Raumplan

- **Raumplan:** rooms are placed by their height, not by floor. Each space gets the level and
  ceiling height its function or importance needs, composed around a central stair.
- **Gradient by height:** public rooms low in the house, private rooms higher up.
- **Two stairs:** a continuous "traditional" stair serving the kitchen side, and a discontinuous
  one reaching the rooms from stepped platforms (servant and served routes kept apart).
- **Entrance:** a low, dark first entry (deep green-blue tiles) opens into a larger, brighter
  cloakroom, then steps up toward the main hall (one source mentions a 1.2 m climb;
  **uncertain**). Pierced walls give diagonal views between levels.
- **Lessons:** level changes as boundaries and as a privacy gradient (our §3 "change of level",
  pushed to the whole house). Severe accessibility cost: a Raumplan house cannot have a step-free
  route.

Sources: https://www.muzeumprahy.cz/en/visit-research-sdcn-architektura/ ,
https://architectuul.com/architecture/villa-muller , https://socks-studio.com/?p=24065

### A7. Alvar Aalto: Villa Mairea (Noormarkku, 1938-39)

- **Plan:** a modified L wrapping a garden with lawn and pool in its inner angle, closed on a
  third side by a wall and the sauna, open to the forest on the fourth. A semi-private enclosure on
  one side, a formal edge toward the approach on the other.
- **Entrance sequence:** under a free-form canopy into a small upper lobby, then through a second
  door into an open hall four steps lower. The foyer deflects movement diagonally toward the
  living room instead of along the axis to dining.
- **Articulation:** a screen of wooden poles and an angled freestanding wall form an informal
  ante-room between living and dining. Ground floor social, upper floor bedrooms and the client's
  studio.
- **Lessons:** the half-enclosed garden is the main "room" the public zone opens to. Light
  boundaries (poles, a step, an angled wall) do the work of a hall.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Mairea , https://www.architecturelab.net/villa-mairea-alvar-aalto/ ,
https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/villa-mairea-noormarkku-1 , https://archeyes.com/villa-mairea-alvar-aalto/

### A8. Luis Barragán: Casa Luis Barragán (Mexico City, 1948)

- **Street:** a closed, plain facade. The house's own site calls the entrance hall a
  "decompression sluice": a pause between street and house, lit with yellow-tinted light, a
  waiting space that prepares the senses (references: convent and Mediterranean thresholds).
- **Second threshold:** a second door separates the dim golden entry from a bright vestibule with
  pink walls, lit indirectly. Volcanic stone paving runs from the entry through the second door, so
  the vestibule feels like an inner courtyard. The colour sequence (yellow, pink, then the green
  garden seen from the dining room) is designed as a sequence.
- **Lesson:** a two-stage entrance (exterior to filter, filter to hall) with changes of light,
  colour and floor. The garden is seen, not entered, from the entry: privacy by controlled view.

Sources: https://casaluisbarragan.org/eng/en_vestibulo.html , https://casaluisbarragan.org/eng/en_porteria.html ,
https://whc.unesco.org/document/151840 , https://daily.jstor.org/casa-luis-barragan-sacred-space-of-mexican-modernism

### A9. Louis Kahn: Fisher House (Hatboro, 1960-67), servant and served

- **Two cubes**, one for living and dining, one for the bedrooms, set at 45 degrees and touching
  at a corner. Public and private are two separate volumes with a single joint.
- **Servant and served:** Kahn's "servant" spaces are stairs, corridors, toilets, kitchens and
  ducts, the spaces that serve the "served" rooms. In Fisher the kitchen is a lower volume set
  inside the double-height living cube so it does not intrude. A third, shed-like volume frames
  the entrance.
- **Lessons:** (1) the private zone as its own volume with one connection point; (2) servant
  spaces grouped and expressed, not scattered between served rooms.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_House_(Hatboro,_Pennsylvania) , https://sah-archipedia.org/node/18343 ,
https://archeyes.com/fisher-house-louis-kahn/ , https://savingplaces.org/twist-of-fate-fisher-kahn-house

### A10. Tadao Ando: Row House in Sumiyoshi / Azuma House (Osaka, 1976)

- A windowless concrete box in a row of houses, split into three equal parts: two enclosed blocks
  with an open courtyard between. The courtyard is circulation: a stair and a bridge cross it, and
  going from the upstairs bedroom to the bathroom means going outdoors. Room positions differ
  between sources (**uncertain**: which bedroom sits over which block).
- All daylight comes from the courtyard; no openings to the street.
- **Lesson:** a deliberate challenge to comfort-first rules. Our §4 allows reaching rooms "from
  outside", so the plan passes our transition rule, but it would fail any "short, sheltered night
  route" rule. Shows that a court can be the transition space.

Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Row_House_in_Sumiyoshi , https://arquitecturaviva.com/works/casa-entre-medianeras ,
https://praemiumimperiale.org/en/laureate/ando , https://www.encyclopedia.com/history/encyclopedias-almanacs-transcripts-and-maps/tadao-ando

### A11. Japanese module (ken, tatami) and Kazuo Shinohara

- **Ken:** about 1,820 mm. Three systems survive: kyo-ma (mats about 1,910 x 955 mm), chu-kyo-ma
  (mats 1,820 x 910 mm, measured between inner post faces) and edo-ma (1,820 mm post centre to
  post centre, mats about 880 x 1,760 mm, sized to fit).
- **Debate:** whether the tatami was ever a true planning module is contested (Engel says no;
  others treat it as one). Room sizes are still named by mat count (4.5, 6, 8 mats).
- **Shinohara, Umbrella House (1961):** a single square room of about 7.5 m, with a floored
  living-dining-kitchen part beside a raised tatami part (15 half-mats) used for sleeping and
  dressing, plus a vestibule. The tatami room serves day and night: use changes by moving futons,
  not by changing rooms.
- **Lessons:** (1) "grid by centre line" vs "grid by clear dimension" is a real choice; our §8
  uses clear dimension (like kyo-ma). (2) Multi-use rooms challenge room typing by furniture.

Sources: https://www.folko.com.au/blog/2023/10/14/japanese-minka-xxii-post-modules ,
https://watanabetaishi.w.waseda.jp/11_A_Floor_for_a_Person_or_Building.pdf ,
https://research.bond.edu.au/en/publications/japanese-module-interpreted-de-quotations-of-re-quotations-on-kat/ ,
https://www.arch.titech.ac.jp/toukakai/TokyoTech/2021-01.html , https://metalocus.es/en/news/kazuo-shinoharas-umbrella-house-reconstructed-vitra-campus

### A12. Glenn Murcutt: Marie Short House (Kempsey, 1974-75)

- Two near-identical pavilions, rotated and slipped: one for sleeping, one for living. Each is
  six timber bays; the last two bays of each are an open entry porch. A thickened wall between
  them implies an external corridor linking the porches and collecting rainwater.
- Wide eaves shade; the house is cross-ventilated; no air conditioning.
- **Lessons:** day and night zones as two buildings; the bay (structural module) sets the plan;
  shallow plans make cross-ventilation and two-sided light automatic.

Sources: https://www.architectmagazine.com/?p=263506 , https://www.architecturelab.net/architect/famous/glenn-murcutt/ ,
https://www.habitusliving.com/series/glenn-murcutt-touch-earth-lightly

### A13. Peter Zumthor: Gugalun House (Versam, 1990-94)

- Extension of a 1706 farmhouse: the old "Stube" (heated living room) is kept, a new annex adds
  kitchen, bathroom, bedroom(s) and heating, all under one new roof. Plan details are thin in web
  sources (bedroom count **uncertain**).
- **Lesson (limited):** the traditional Stube is a heated core room; services are added as a
  separate, clearly new layer. Little plan evidence to build a check on.

Sources: https://www.vibrarch.upv.es/?p=3464 , https://www.dezeen.com/2009/04/18/key-projects-by-peter-zumthor/

### A14. Eileen Gray: E-1027 (Roquebrune-Cap-Martin, 1926-29)

- Gray rejected the uninterrupted open plan of her peers: she used definite planes and
  obstructions so rooms are revealed gradually and stay private. Her own 1929 diagram (in
  L'Architecture Vivante) traces the movement routes through the house with arrows.
- Ribbon and concertina windows open to the sea; rolling shutters and canvas screen the interior
  from view.
- **Lessons:** a designer mapping routes as a design tool (exactly what our room graph does);
  privacy by screens and angled planes, not only by distance.

Sources: https://docomomojournal.com/index.php/journal/article/download/342/94/94 ,
https://www.nzia.co.nz/awards/warren-trust-awards-for-architectural-writing/2019-writing-awards/eileen-gray-and-e1027/ ,
https://c20society.org.uk/building-of-the-month/e-1027-roquebrune-cap-martin-france

### A15. Charles and Ray Eames: Case Study House 8 (Pacific Palisades, 1949)

- Steel frame of bays 20 ft x 7 ft 4 in (6.10 x 2.24 m), 17 ft high. Residence 3 bays x 8 bays
  (one bay an overhang for the rear patio), a court 4 bays wide, then a 5-bay studio. About
  1,500 sq ft residence plus 1,000 sq ft studio.
- The two volumes are in line, separated by an open court; the plan was rotated 90 degrees to
  keep the meadow.
- **Lessons:** work and home separated by an outdoor room; every dimension is a bay count.

Sources: https://npgallery.nps.gov/GetAsset/69014eb9-9eb2-491a-910e-a3e9d3414a58 ,
https://sah-archipedia.org/node/49186 , https://www.eamesinstitute.org/stories/case-study-house-8

### A16. Paulo Mendes da Rocha: Casa Butanta (Sao Paulo, 1964)

- A raised concrete slab on few supports (four pillars in at least one description; **uncertain**
  for this house). Interior partitions do not reach the ceiling, so space and sound are continuous;
  acoustic privacy is given up on purpose.
- One description has the living area wrapped around the bedrooms in a U, the bedrooms behind a
  long common space (**uncertain** which house it describes).
- **Lesson:** an explicit trade of acoustic privacy for spatial continuity; in our §3 table,
  "partition below ceiling" sits between curtain and heavy wall.

Sources: https://architecturehunter.com/project/butanta-house-paulo-mendes-da-rocha/ ,
https://docomomobrasil.com/wp-content/uploads/2016/08/OBR_53.pdf ,
https://comum.rcaap.pt/bitstreams/9264d73c-0165-4e55-bb8c-7356fdefa00e/download

### A17. Ecuadorian modern houses (Quito)

- Milton Barragan Dumet built many single-family houses in the 1960s, including his own Casa del
  Arquitecto (1962), on a long narrow plot with a front and a back patio; also Casa de la Colina
  (1966) and Casa Muller (1963-64). Ovidio Wappenstein's Casa Ordonez (1977) is described as a more
  intimate, vernacular approach in brick and wood.
- No plans were found online. The sources to obtain are *Miradas a la arquitectura moderna en el
  Ecuador* (Hermida, 2010) and *Casas y arquitectos modernos en Quito* (CARQ-USFQ).
- **Lesson (tentative):** front and back patios on a narrow plot are the local way to give every
  room light and to place the private zone at the back. Worth a follow-up with the books, as these
  are the most relevant precedents for Quito plots.

Sources: https://dialnet.unirioja.es/descarga/articulo/5231500.pdf ,
https://libros.usfq.edu.ec/index.php/usfqpress/catalog/download/54/103/237?inline=1 ,
https://primicias.ec/entretenimiento/cultura/milton-barragan-arquitectura-escutura-arte-75896

### A18. Existenzminimum, the Frankfurt kitchen and Alexander Klein

- **CIAM 2 (Frankfurt, 1929), "Die Wohnung fur das Existenzminimum":** plans from many countries
  shown at one scale so they could be compared. Ernst May's Praunheim plan, with the
  Schutte-Lihotzky kitchen, was singled out.
- **Frankfurt kitchen (1926-27):** about 3.44 x 1.90 m, with a working aisle of only about 95 cm,
  laid out from time-and-motion studies of cooking and cleaning sequences. A compact, separate work
  room next to the living room.
- **Alexander Klein (presented 1928, CIAM 1929):** graphic methods to evaluate plans objectively,
  including diagrams of the occupants' movement. **Crossing routes are a functional error;** the
  remedy is a hall that separates the day zone from the night zone so each is reached
  independently, with no crossing between day routes and night routes.
- **Lessons:** our method is in this tradition (objective comparison at one scale). Klein's
  route-crossing test is directly computable on our room graph.

Sources: https://www.moma.org/collection/works/6107 , https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_kitchen ,
https://collections.vam.ac.uk/item/O121079 , https://austria-forum.org/web-books/en/austrianarchitect00en2018isds/000024 ,
https://www.saraivaeassociados.com/alexander-klein-and-the-modern-movement-the-exacerbation-of-functional-predetermination/ ,
https://www.cogitatiopress.com/urbanplanning/article/view/2157

## Part B. Synthesis

### B1. Lessons that confirm our principles

| Our principle | Confirmed by |
|---|---|
| §2 zones: public at the entrance, private away and separate | Jacobs L (bedroom wing), Fisher (two cubes), Marie Short (two pavilions), Mairea (bedrooms upstairs), Klein (day and night zones), Alexander 127 intimacy gradient |
| §2 service zone with its own supply | Savoye (car and staff on the ground floor), Loos (separate service stair), Kahn (servant spaces) |
| §2 rule 1: bedrooms not off the central public space | Klein (night zone reached independently of the day zone), Alexander 136 (couple's realm) |
| §3 light boundaries define spaces | Robie (fireplace mass), Farnsworth (core as furniture), Tugendhat (onyx wall), Mairea (pole screen, four steps), Umbrella House (raised tatami floor) |
| §4 entrance into a transition space | Barragan ("decompression sluice"), Loos (low dark entry then cloakroom), Robie and Fallingwater (compressed entry), Mairea (lobby then hall), Alexander 112 |
| §4 never through a useful room | Klein (crossing routes are an error), Gray (mapped routes) |
| §5 space economy, little circulation | Existenzminimum, Frankfurt kitchen, Usonian (no parlour, gallery doubling as storage) |
| §8 planning module | Jacobs (2 x 4 ft), Eames (7 ft 4 in bays), Savoye (4.75 m grid), ken / tatami, Murcutt (timber bays), Modulor |

### B2. Lessons that add to or challenge our principles

1. **Open public zone (challenge to §4).** Farnsworth, Robie, Jacobs, Tugendhat and Umbrella House
   have living, dining and often kitchen as one space with no hall between them. Our method
   already allows this through §3 (same-zone light boundaries make one space). The challenge is
   real only for the entrance: Fallingwater and Jacobs enter almost directly into the living room,
   with the "transition" reduced to a low vestibule or a compressed passage. Candidate rule: accept
   a small entry niche (porch outside plus a low or narrow zone inside) as a portal, by size, even
   if it is part of the living room outline.
2. **Transition by section, not by room (adds to §4).** Compression and release (Wright), the
   Raumplan steps (Loos), the four steps down at Mairea: the transition is made by ceiling height,
   level and light. Candidate rule: the entrance space is lower (ceiling) or narrower than the main
   living space it leads to.
3. **Two-stage entrance (adds to §4).** Barragan and Mairea use two doors or two thresholds:
   exterior, filter, hall. Alexander 112 puts the first stage outside (porch, change of surface or
   level). Candidate rule: a covered transition outside the entrance door.
4. **Intimacy gradient (formalises §2).** Our zones are categories; Alexander 127 and Loos make
   them an order. Candidate rule: graph depth from the entrance increases with privacy.
5. **Private zone with one joint (sharpens §2 rule 5).** Fisher, Jacobs and Marie Short connect
   the private wing to the public one at a single point. Candidate rule: count the connections
   between private and public subgraphs.
6. **Day and night routes do not cross (Klein; sharpens §4).** Candidate rule: the night route
   (bedroom to bathroom) stays in the private zone and private circulation.
7. **Servant and served (Kahn; adds a new idea).** Wet and service spaces grouped into a core or
   strip (Farnsworth core, Jacobs hinge, Fisher kitchen box). Candidate rule: wet rooms cluster
   (plumbing economy).
8. **Light on two sides (Alexander 159; adds to daylight).** Our current daylight work follows
   the code (window area). Candidate rule: main rooms lit from two orientations, or shallow.
9. **Private outdoor space and garden relation (Fallingwater, Mairea, Eames, Alexander 111).**
   Candidate rule: living or dining opens directly onto an outdoor space on the side away from
   the road.
10. **Alcoves (Alexander 179; challenge to §5 "all space usable").** A niche is not waste when it
    holds a use (window seat, bed alcove, desk bay). Our shape check already exempts niches wider
    than 600 mm; candidate refinement: a niche with a furniture piece in it counts as used.
11. **Level changes (Loos; challenge to accessibility).** Our §3 treats a level change as a
    walkable boundary. A Raumplan house would pass our checks and still fail anyone in a
    wheelchair. Candidate rule: a step-free route from the entrance to living, kitchen, one
    bathroom and one bedroom.
12. **Section beats plan distance for zoning (Tugendhat; challenge to §2 siting).** Bedrooms on
    the road side but on another floor are private. Candidate rule: in multi-storey dwellings,
    distance from the road counts the floor difference (or the siting test runs per floor).
13. **Multi-use rooms (Umbrella House, tatami; challenge to §6).** Typing a room by fixed
    furniture assumes one use per room. Low priority for Quito housing; note only.
14. **Outdoor circulation (Ando, Murcutt; edge of §4).** Our §4 allows "from outside". Candidate
    refinement: an outdoor link counts as circulation only if covered.
15. **Module as a series or a structural grid (Modulor, Eames, ken; adds to §8).** Our module
    tests clear dimensions. Precedents tie walls to a structural grid on centre lines, and
    Modulor allows a series of preferred sizes. Candidate: optional grid-alignment mode (wall
    axes on a grid; for timber panel construction a 600 or 1200 mm grid is the natural one).
16. **Zen view and discretion (Alexander 134, Barragan, Gray).** Views and privacy controlled by
    what is seen from where. Candidate rule: no bathroom or bedroom door visible from the entrance
    door (this is our §2 rule 2 "discretion", currently not checked).
17. **Kitchen as a work room (Frankfurt) vs kitchen as the hinge (Usonian).** Both put the kitchen
    next to dining with a short link; Frankfurt shows a 95 cm aisle is workable for one person, but
    current norms use about 1,200 mm. Candidate: kitchen-dining adjacency and aisle width.

### B3. Candidate rules as checks

Rating: **value** = how much it improves design quality for our dwellings; **effort** = work on
top of what exists (room graph, zones, `at.roomReach`, door and window data).
Existing tools that some candidates extend: `ATZONECHECK`, `ATPASSAGECHECK`, `ATDAYLIGHTCHECK`,
`ATROOMDEPTHCHECK`, `ATKITCHENCHECK`, `ATROOMSHAPECHECK`, `ATMODULECHECK`, `ATSITECHECK`.

### B4. Recommended order

1. **C1 intimacy gradient, C2 one joint, C3 night route:** all use the existing room graph and
   zone tags, encode Klein and Alexander precisely, and catch real plan errors. Add to
   `ATZONECHECK` / `ATDWELLINGCHECK`.
2. **C4 light on two sides and C5 garden side:** extend daylight and site checks; high design
   value, data already in the drawing (windows, road marks).
3. **C9 step-free route and C11 section-aware siting:** prevent the method from passing a plan
   that fails accessibility, and from failing a good hillside plan.
4. **C6 wet core, C12 kitchen link:** cheap, practical, cost-related.
5. Later or guidance only: C10 visibility (most effort), C7, C8, C13 to C18.

### B5. Open points

- Kahn's general definition of servant spaces (stairs, corridors, toilets, ducts) is taken from the
  Fisher House sources above; his own texts (Trenton Bath House, Richards Laboratories) were not
  fetched in this session.
- Ecuadorian plans (Milton Barragan, Wappenstein) need the CARQ-USFQ and Hermida books; they are
  the closest precedents to our Quito work and could ground C5 (front and back patios) locally.
- Thresholds marked "to confirm" (C4 depth rule, C12 aisle) must be checked against Neufert and the
  Quito ordinance before they become PROBLEMs; until then they report "check".
